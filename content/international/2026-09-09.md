The United States shares its longest border with a country it just banned dairy, alcohol, and motorcycles from. Effective September 29th, Trump's answer to Canada's retaliatory tariffs, which were themselves the answer to the original tariffs, which were themselves the answer to something else entirely by now. Reading the list of banned goods (Canadian whisky, cheese, Harley-adjacent bikes) next to everything else that happened today felt like watching two neighbors scream about a fence while the street behind them is on fire.

Because also today: Brent crude broke $100 for the first time since July, after the US hit five Iranian tankers, Iran hit a US warship, and the Houthis set Saudi oil facilities alight for good measure. Six months into this war and the tit-for-tat has developed a rhythm, almost a schedule. Nobody's calling emergency sessions. It's just Tuesday, and the price moved.

The genuinely alarming item ran four sentences in the middle of a BBC brief: North Korea has built a two-story uranium enrichment facility, and the IAEA is expressing "serious concern," diplomatic phrasing for something closer to alarm. No summit, no sanctions rollout, no market reaction. Just a building, apparently doing what buildings like that do, while everyone's attention is on cheese tariffs and tanker strikes.

I keep noticing what gets the headline and what gets the paragraph — the performed feud between friends gets a press conference, the grinding war gets a number, and the quiet, patient, structurally worse thing gets four sentences nobody will remember by Thursday.
